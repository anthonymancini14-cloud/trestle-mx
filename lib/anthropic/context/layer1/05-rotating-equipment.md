# Rotating equipment repair practice

General good practice. Manufacturer instructions and the site's own tolerances govern.

## Vibration and bearing condition

- ISO 20816 (formerly ISO 10816) frames vibration severity by machine class and mounting; sites usually set alert and alarm levels from their own baselines. Rising trend matters as much as the absolute value.
- Bearing defect frequencies, rising high-frequency energy and rising bearing temperature together point to bearing distress. Misalignment, looseness and lubrication problems are frequent root causes; replacing a bearing without finding the cause invites a repeat.
- Oil analysis (wear metals, particle count, water) and the condition of drained oil are useful evidence.

## Bearing replacement

- Mount with an induction heater or press; never strike the rolling elements or the cage.
- Measure shaft and housing fits when a bearing has failed or spun.
- Inspect the removed bearing and keep it for failure analysis; photograph the races.
- Confirm lubricant type and fill level for the housing after assembly.

## Mechanical seals

- If a pump is opened for bearings and the seal is already weeping, replacing the seal in the same outage is usually cheaper than a second outage.
- Cartridge seals: set the setting clips correctly and remove them before start-up. Never run a seal dry.
- Check shaft runout and sleeve condition at the seal.

## Alignment

- Check and correct soft foot before aligning: a common practice is to correct any foot showing more than about 0.002 in (0.05 mm) of lift.
- Use laser alignment where available. Typical published tolerances for flexible couplings tighten with speed; at 1,800 rpm a common "excellent" target is offset within about 0.002 in and angularity within about 0.5 mils per inch. Use the site or OEM table.
- Record as-found and as-left readings in the work order.

## Lifting

- Know the component weight (casing halves, rotors, motors) before choosing hoists and rigging. Under-sized lifting equipment is a frequent cause of delay.
